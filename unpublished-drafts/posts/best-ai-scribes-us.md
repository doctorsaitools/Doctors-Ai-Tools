An AI medical scribe listens to a consultation and drafts the note for you to check and sign. If you practise in the US, you can now choose from tools built for a solo clinician at one end and for a whole health system at the other.

This page is a shortlist to trial, not a ranking. **Status: researched, not yet tested.** Everything below comes from each vendor's own pages and named industry sources, checked on 17 October 2026. We will rank these tools only after we have tested them on realistic consultations and Dr. Gupta has reviewed the results. Read how we work on the [methodology page](/methodology/).

## Where to start, by situation

- **Solo clinician or small practice:** start with a trial of Freed or Heidi. Freed lists a paid entry plan from US$39 a month with a 7-day free trial and no card. Heidi has a free plan, so you can see how scribing fits your day before paying.
- **Multilingual or multispecialty group:** look at Nabla, which lists a browser extension, APIs and several security certifications.
- **Hospital, health system or large group:** the enterprise tools are Abridge, Suki and Microsoft Dragon Copilot. None of them publishes a price, so expect a sales process.

## How we chose these six

We started from the scribes named in independent roundups such as Unite.ai and on our own [AI scribes hub](/ai-scribes/), and kept the ones sold to US clinicians. We left out Lyrebird Health and Tali, which are built mainly for Australia and Canada. Ambience and DeepScribe, two enterprise and specialty tools, will be added once we have researched them.

We did not test any of them. A vendor's description of itself is a starting point, not a finding, and every claim below is attributed to the vendor.

## Freed

Freed is aimed at individual clinicians and small practices. The pricing page shows four tiers. Starter allows up to 40 notes a month. Core and above allow unlimited notes. Premier adds visit summaries, patient instructions, EHR push and ICD-10 and CPT coding suggestions. Groups adds shared notes, single sign-on and admin dashboards.

Freed describes itself as HIPAA-compliant and SOC 2 Type II certified. Its pricing page lists an organisation-wide business associate agreement (BAA) under Groups, so an individual doctor should ask how a BAA is handled on a single-user plan. Read our full [Freed review](/ai-scribes/freed-review/).

## Heidi

Heidi sells to everyone from a single clinician to enterprise teams. The US pricing page shows a Free plan with one seat and unlimited transcription with standard templates, a Clinician plan with a 14-day trial, and a Teams plan on request. The free plan limits some extras such as coding and evidence answers. The page does not show a price for the Clinician plan, so check Heidi's site. Heidi is also listed on the NHS England supplier registry, which matters if you work across borders (see [our NHS guide](/ai-scribes/nhs-ambient-voice-technology-guidance/)).

## Nabla

Nabla offers ambient notes, dictation, templates and patient instructions, with a browser extension and APIs. Unite.ai lists HIPAA, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and GDPR-aligned infrastructure, and configurable retention. We could not load Nabla's own pricing page when we checked, so confirm current plans with the vendor.

## Suki

Suki positions itself as a voice-first assistant for documentation, orders and coding across more than 100 specialties. It names Epic, Oracle Health, athenahealth and MEDITECH as its deepest EHR integrations and says it is SOC 2 Type 2 certified and HIPAA compliant. It publishes outcome figures such as time saved on notes, but without the method, sample size or period, so we do not repeat them here. No pricing is published.

## Abridge

Abridge targets large health systems. It says it is trusted by more than 300 health systems and handles more than 100 million conversations a year. Those are the vendor's figures. Its home page does not state pricing, EHR integrations or compliance certifications, so ask for them in writing during a demo.

## Microsoft Dragon Copilot

Dragon Copilot combines ambient capture with medical dictation, draft notes, referral letters and patient summaries, with desktop, web and mobile apps. It is licensed per user through Microsoft partners, and licensing changed in April 2026, so any older price you have seen may be out of date. It suits organisations already on Microsoft or Dragon products.

## What to ask every vendor

1. **Will you sign a BAA?** Under HIPAA, a vendor that handles patient information for you is a business associate. Ask for the agreement before the first real patient.
2. **Where do recordings and transcripts go, and for how long?** Freed's pricing page says audio is saved temporarily and then deleted, and also says it does not store recordings. Ask any vendor to state its rule in one clear sentence.
3. **Does it write into your EHR, or do you copy and paste?** Copy and paste is fine for a solo practice. Groups usually need notes in the record.
4. **What does it cost after the trial?** Look for note caps, annual-only prices and per-user fees.
5. **How much editing does each note need?** A scribe only saves time if you trust the draft. Test it on your own real consultations.

## Consent and your responsibility

Tell patients that the consultation is being recorded by an AI tool and get their agreement before you start. Rules on recording differ by state, and your employer or hospital may have its own policy, so check both.

The AI drafts. You review, correct and sign. You stay responsible for the note.
