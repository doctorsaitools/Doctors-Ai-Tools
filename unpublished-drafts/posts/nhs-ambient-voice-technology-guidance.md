If you are a GP or practice manager in England and you want to use an AI scribe, NHS England has told you what to do first. This page explains that guidance in plain English: what it covers, the steps it asks you to take, and what the supplier registry does and does not mean.

This is a summary, not legal advice. The guidance applies to England only, and we read Version 2 of it on 17 October 2026. Always check the current text before you act.

## What the guidance covers

NHS England's guidance on AI-enabled ambient scribing products is written for chief information officers and chief clinical information officers, with a technical appendix for product and technical teams. It covers:

- ambient scribes, also called ambient voice technologies (AVT), that turn speech into text and then produce summaries, letters, clinical codes or record entries
- large language model plug-ins added to existing documentation tools

It does not cover unauthorised tools that staff use outside the organisation's supervision. That matters, because an unsupervised tool sits outside every safeguard below.

## The supplier registry

On 19 January 2026, NHS England launched a registry of suppliers of ambient voice technology. Nineteen suppliers were named at launch, including Accurx, Corti, eConsult, Heidi Health, Lyrebird Health, Microsoft Dragon, Optum (EMIS), Tortus and X-On Health.

Three points about what the registry is:

- **Suppliers are self-certified.** They have supplied evidence against the criteria, and NHS England says it has carried out only preliminary checks.
- **It is not a commercial framework.** It is a reference list, not a procurement route.
- **The decision stays with you.** Practices still run their own procurement and their own safety and data protection checks.

Evidence supplied by vendors can be viewed through the NHS England National Commercial and Procurement Hub.

## The quick implementation guide

The guidance sets out five early steps.

1. **Appoint a clinical safety officer.** This person leads the identification of technical and clinical risks.
2. **Complete DCB0160 and a DPIA.** DCB0160 is the clinical safety standard for organisations deploying health IT. It means a safety case, a hazard log and a monitoring plan. A data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is also required. NHS England says templates are still being developed.
3. **Plan integration.** For general practice, think about how the scribe works with your principal electronic patient record.
4. **Set controls in procurement.** Suppliers should have completed the Digital Technology Assessment Criteria (DTAC) and should hold at least MHRA Class 1 registration. Users must review and approve outputs before anything further happens, and staff must be trained.
5. **Monitor.** Audit the accuracy of outputs, review incidents and performance, and check the supplier's post-market surveillance.

## Is the scribe a medical device?

It depends on what it does, and the guidance says the status depends on the product's intended purpose and functionality.

- Simple transcription is likely not a medical device.
- Summarisation using generative AI, clinical codes, prompts that call for action, or diagnostic or treatment suggestions are likely to make the product a device.

A device needs MHRA registration and either a UKCA certificate or a CE mark. The guidance says a CE mark remains valid until 30 June 2028. For the registry, suppliers must hold at least MHRA Class 1 registration. Ask each supplier which class it holds and why.

## Who does what

| Task | Supplier | Your practice |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical safety case (DCB0129) | Completes it | Asks to see it |
| Deployment safety documents (DCB0160) | Supports | Completes them |
| DTAC | Completes it | Checks it in procurement |
| MHRA registration | Holds it, at least Class 1 | Checks the class matches the product's function |
| DPIA | Supplies information | Completes it |
| Reviewing each note | Not applicable | Does it every time |

## The jargon in one line each

- **DCB0129:** the clinical risk management standard that suppliers follow.
- **DCB0160:** the matching standard for the organisation that deploys the product.
- **DTAC:** the Digital Technology Assessment Criteria, NHS England's baseline checks for digital health suppliers.
- **DPIA:** a data protection impact assessment, required under UK GDPR for higher-risk processing.
- **DSPT and Cyber Essentials:** the data security toolkit and the UK government cyber security certification that the guidance expects.
- **UKCA:** the UK conformity mark for medical devices. The guidance says CE marks stay valid until 30 June 2028.

## Consent, transparency and the patient

The guidance asks you to be open about what is recorded, what the output is, who uses it and how it is stored. In practice:

- update your privacy notice
- tell patients before the consultation so they can object
- be clear about your legal basis, and whether patient consent is needed
- watch for automated decision-making rules under UK GDPR Article 22

The guidance also asks you to monitor for bias, for example with accents and speech disorders.

## You still sign the note

Users must review and approve every output, and practitioners remain responsible for checking and correcting it. The guidance also notes that NHS organisations may keep liability under a non-delegable duty of care. A supplier's contract does not remove your responsibility.

Care Quality Commission regulation applies to your service whether or not you use the technology.

## Pilots

The guidance says pilots should be limited to four months and must not be used to bypass compliance. Finish the safety work first, then pilot.

## Ten questions to ask a supplier

1. Are you on the NHS England registry, and what evidence did you submit?
2. Which MHRA class are you registered under, and why?
3. Have you completed DCB0129 and DTAC? Can we see the documents?
4. Where are audio, transcripts and notes stored, and for how long?
5. Which UK GDPR role do you take, and who are your sub-processors?
6. Which EPRs do you integrate with, and how?
7. How do you test for accent and speech bias?
8. How do you handle errors, incidents and post-market surveillance?
9. What does the contract say about liability?
10. What happens to our notes if we leave?

## What about Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

This guidance is for England. If you practise elsewhere in the UK, check what your own health service says.
